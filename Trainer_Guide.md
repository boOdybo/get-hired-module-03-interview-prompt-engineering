# Module 03 — Trainer Guide & Implementation Notes

## Teaching purpose

This module teaches students to use ChatGPT as an interview-preparation assistant while preserving ownership of their real experience. The instructional centre is not “AI writes a perfect interview answer”; it is:

**Student's real experience → STAR structure → advanced prompt → AI draft → critical comparison → iterative refinement → saved prompt bank.**

Use the deck's three modes:
- **Presentation:** large projected content, diagrams, and guided reveal.
- **Study:** expanded explanations and reminders.
- **Trainer:** slide-specific facilitation notes.

No content should be removed merely to fit a clock. The source's sequence and the student's evidence of learning determine how deeply each concept is taught.

## Before teaching

1. Ask students to prepare one real experience from university, volunteering, part-time work, a personal project, or another appropriate context.
2. Remind students that formal employment is not required for a valid STAR example.
3. Use fictional/anonymised data in the trainer's demonstration.
4. Confirm that students can access ChatGPT. Do not make a paid feature a requirement.
5. Prepare an offline sample prompt and sample answer, clearly labelled as a simulation, in case access fails.
6. Explain that students should not enter passwords, confidential employer information, or sensitive personal data.

## Core teaching sequence

### 1. Why storytelling matters
Explain that behavioural questions seek evidence of behaviour. Contrast a generic claim (“I am a good problem solver”) with a specific, defensible example.

### 2. Teach STAR in depth
- **Situation:** the minimum context needed to understand the challenge.
- **Task:** the candidate's responsibility, distinct from the team's goal.
- **Action:** the candidate's specific steps and decisions. This is often the most revealing part.
- **Result:** a truthful outcome, qualitative or quantitative, plus learning where relevant.

Do not require every student to have a dramatic achievement or a numeric result. A modest but honest example is better than an invented success metric.

### 3. Model one STAR answer
Use the fictional university project example in the deck. State explicitly that it is fictional. Identify each STAR segment and discuss how a follow-up question might test the answer.

### 4. Explain the advanced prompt anatomy
The source's five elements are:
- Role
- Context
- Tone
- Format
- Constraint

Clarify that this is the Module 03 teaching framework, extending Module 01's Role + Context + Task + Format. Do not present the five-part formula as a universal rule for every prompt.

### 5. Live demonstration standard
The live demo should show:
1. The interview question and desired outcome.
2. The exact prompt, including editable placeholders.
3. The context supplied by the student/trainer.
4. The AI output.
5. What the output does well.
6. What is generic, assumed, unsupported, or missing.
7. A targeted follow-up prompt.
8. The revised output and the human review decision.

Do not simply paste a prompt and accept the first response.

### 6. Compare AI output with the student's own answer
Ask students to draft their own answer before using AI. Compare:
- Structure: is STAR recognisable?
- Specificity: are the actions concrete?
- Authenticity: does it reflect what actually happened?
- Evidence: are results supported?
- Speakability: can the student say and explain it naturally?

AI may improve organisation and language while losing authentic details. The student's own facts remain authoritative.

### 7. Iterative prompting
Use one targeted improvement at a time. Examples:
- Ask questions to clarify the student's role.
- Strengthen the Action section using only supplied actions.
- Flag unsupported claims or assumptions.
- Make the answer sound natural when spoken.

Avoid vague instructions such as “make it better” unless students are being shown why they are weak.

### 8. Question bank and organisation
Preserve the source prompt:
`What are the 10 most common behavioral interview questions for [sector]?`

Explain that AI-generated lists are practice suggestions, not a verified statistical ranking. Encourage students to organise questions by competency and save prompt versions, STAR stories, and practice notes.

### 9. Prompt Duel
Follow the source activity:
1. Pair students.
2. Give both pairs the same interview question and equivalent context.
3. Each pair writes a competing prompt.
4. Run both prompts in ChatGPT.
5. Display outputs side by side.
6. Guild members vote for the more useful answer.
7. The winning participant explains the prompting strategy.

Suggested trainer-designed criteria (not official XP):
- Clarity of the task.
- Adequacy of context.
- Use of STAR.
- Authenticity constraints.
- Usability as a spoken answer.

Do not award a win simply because a prompt is longer.

## Student activities and evidence

| Activity | Student action | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| STAR dissection | Identify S/T/A/R and diagnose a weak section | Annotated answer |
| Personal STAR draft | Write from a real experience before AI | Original answer |
| Prompt construction | Complete the five-part framework | Advanced prompt |
| AI review | Compare own answer and AI draft | Review notes |
| Iterative refinement | Write a targeted follow-up prompt | Before/after versions |
| Prompt Duel | Compete, compare, vote, explain | Two prompts, outputs, vote rationale |
| Prompt Bank | Save at least five prompts | Downloaded TXT or equivalent file |

## Assessment guidance

Assess process and judgement, not only polish:
- Is the story real and attributable to the student?
- Is STAR structure clear?
- Does the prompt include relevant context and constraints?
- Did the student identify an unsupported claim?
- Did the student improve the output for a stated reason?
- Can the student explain and speak the final answer?

### Suggested Prompt Duel practice rubric (trainer-designed)
Each criterion can receive 0–2 points:
1. Task clarity.
2. Context quality.
3. STAR structure.
4. Authenticity constraint.
5. Output usability.

This 10-point practice rubric is not an official XP scale. The source only states that the winning participant earns XP.

## Homework — Interview Preparation Pack

This is an additional take-home task, not an extra official daily deliverable:
1. Select one real experience and write a STAR story.
2. Attach the original prompt and refined prompt.
3. Attach the AI draft and mark unsupported assumptions.
4. Produce a final answer the student can speak naturally.
5. Add two likely follow-up questions.
6. Save the work in an Interview Preparation folder.

Minimum completion: real story, two prompt versions, at least one documented human correction, and a speakable final answer.

## Daily deliverable attribution

Keep the PDF's daily deliverables separate:
- STAR Answer Card (3 complete behavioural answers): storytelling/soft-skills work.
- AI-refined interview prompt bank (at least 5 prompts saved): **AI Tools direct deliverable**.
- 60-second “My Challenge” audio: Business English.
- Language journal entry (10 experience verbs and phrases): Business English.

The AI session can support these other outputs, but must not claim ownership of all of them.

## Troubleshooting and fallback

- **No ChatGPT access:** students still write prompts and assess a prepared simulation. Label the simulation clearly.
- **No formal work experience:** use a genuine university, volunteering, personal-project, or community example.
- **Missing result:** state what is known and what was learned; do not fabricate metrics.
- **Students copy AI answers:** require them to explain every sentence and answer a follow-up question.
- **Weak prompt duel comparison:** make sure both pairs received the same question and comparable context.
- **AI generates a fake statistic or achievement:** remove it and discuss why polished language does not make it true.
